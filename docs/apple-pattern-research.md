# Apple.com pattern research

## Source: Apple homepage

URL: https://www.apple.com/

Apple's public homepage uses a compact global navigation with product-family links, dedicated search and shopping-bag controls, and explicit accessible names such as “Search apple.com” and “Shopping Bag.” The hero presents a concise headline, a short supporting sentence, and one primary Shop CTA. Product sections repeat a restrained Learn more / Buy pair rather than turning every action into a filled button. Media galleries expose tab-like item controls and a labeled play/pause control. The footer is dense but grouped into clear information-architecture columns covering shopping, services, account, business, education, values, support, and legal content.

Adaptation decision for TechStore: preserve the existing compact header and route scope, improve search/bag naming and keyboard states, keep a single above-fold primary CTA, retain Learn more / Buy hierarchy, and use grouped footer accordions. Do not copy Apple's brand content or add product/service categories that do not exist in TechStore.

## Source: Apple iPhone overview

URL: https://www.apple.com/iphone/

Apple's product-family overview pairs a chapter navigation strip with a clear “Explore the lineup” heading, a compare-all link, and repeated product tiles. Each tile presents product name, image, finishes, concise benefit-led copy, price/financing context, and a consistent Learn more / Buy action pair. Supporting commercial reassurance is grouped later into Apple Upgrade, Trade In, Apple Card, delivery/pickup, personal setup, and guided shopping. Compatibility is expressed as compact accordion sections such as iPhone and Mac, iPhone and Apple Watch, and iPhone and AirPods rather than a wall of copy. The page also provides a media gallery with explicit tab/button labels and accessible next-item controls.

Adaptation decision for TechStore: use a compact category strip and compare link on collection pages, strengthen product cards with concise benefit/price/availability hierarchy, keep financing/trade-in language truthful and labeled as demo or unavailable where appropriate, and expose compatibility through collapsible sections. Do not invent Apple claims, financing programs, or product names for TechStore.

## Source: Apple Store iPhone configuration

URL: https://www.apple.com/us/shop/buy-iphone/iphone-17-pro

Apple's buying flow keeps the product title and price context visible at the top, then separates choices into deliberate configuration groups such as model, finish, storage, and connectivity. It surfaces trade-in and payment explanations as adjacent, expandable reassurance rather than burying them in checkout. The page includes a “Need help choosing a model?” support prompt, an image gallery with labeled next controls, and a Frequently Asked Questions accordion covering activation, compatibility, returns, and setup. The footer remains consistent with the broader Apple information architecture.

Adaptation decision for TechStore: make configuration choices easy to scan, keep price and availability adjacent to the selection area, label demo payment and delivery limitations clearly, add contextual help or FAQ content where the product data supports it, and avoid implying real trade-in, financing, activation, or return programs that the static demo cannot provide.
